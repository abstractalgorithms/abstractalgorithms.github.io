LLM Hyperparameters Guide: Temperature, Top-P, and Top-K Explained

Scenario context

Temperature, Top-p, and Top-k are three sampling controls that determine how "creative" or "deterministic" an LLM's output is. Temperature rescales the probability distribution; Top-k limits the candidate pool by count; Top-p limits it by cumulative probability. Understanding all three — and when to combine them — is essential for production LLM work.

This walkthrough focuses on The Three Knobs Behind Every LLM Response.

Before making a design choice, clarify:

  • Think of sampling controls as three dials on a mixing board: - Temperature — the creativity dial.
  • Turn it up for surprising outputs; turn it down for safe, predictable ones.
  • It reshapes the entire probability distribution before any other control runs.

Stage 1 — The Basics: What Sampling Parameters Control

  • After an LLM finishes processing your prompt, it outputs a probability distribution over its entire vocabulary — potentially 50,000+ tokens.
  • Sampling parameters control how you draw a single token from that distribution.
  • The three parameters and their roles: - Temperature — scales the probability distribution.
  • Low values concentrate probability on the top tokens; high values spread it out.

Stage 2 — Temperature: Sharpening or Flattening the Probability Curve

  • After the model computes raw scores (logits) for each candidate token, it applies a softmax to get probabilities.
  • Temperature $T$ scales the logits before softmax: $$P i = rac{\exp(z i / T)}{\sum j \exp(z j / T)}$$ Concrete example — logits for three tokens: Mat=2.0 , Rug=1.5 , Moon=0.2 At T=0.5, "Mat" wins 73% of the time — focuse…
  • At T=2.0, "Moon" steals 20% — surprising.

Stage 3 — Top-k: Hard Cap on the Candidate Pool

  • Top-k restricts sampling to only the k highest-probability next tokens.
  • After Temperature scaling, only the top k tokens are kept; all others are set to zero probability (and renormalized).
  • Trade-off: Top-k is a static threshold.
  • If the distribution is wide (many roughly equal options), k=10 may still allow incoherent tokens.

Stage 4 — Top-p (Nucleus Sampling): Dynamic Candidate Pool by Mass

  • Top-p is more adaptive — it includes the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches p .
  • The key word is cumulative : the candidate set size changes dynamically.
  • If the model is very confident, nucleus may be just 1–2 tokens.
  • If uncertain, it may include 50+.

Stage 5 — How Token Sampling Flows: The Complete Pipeline

  • Key observation: Temperature, Top-k, and Top-p are applied in sequence, not simultaneously.
  • Temperature reshapes the entire distribution first; Top-k then hard-caps the candidates; Top-p further filters by cumulative probability.
  • Each step makes the sampling more constrained.

Stage 6 — How Temperature, Top-k, and Top-p Interact

  • These three controls are typically applied in sequence: Temperature → Top-k → Top-p.
  • Interaction gotcha: Setting both Top-k and Top-p is redundant in most cases — whichever is more restrictive wins.
  • Most practitioners use either temperature + top-p OR temperature alone (at T=0 for deterministic tasks).
