Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3
Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees.

Geospatial indexing maps 2D (lat, lon) coordinates into 1D cell IDs for fast proximity lookups; Uber H3 uses hexagonal cells so every neighbor is equidistant, eliminating square-grid corner distortion.

Why Static SQL B-Trees & R-Trees Fail at 1M GPS Pings/Sec.

Why this matters: Diagnose why disk-backed 2D R-trees choke when millions of drivers update coordinates every 4 seconds.

Think of Why Static SQL B-Trees & R-Trees Fail at 1M GPS Pings/Sec as the intuition layer for Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3 Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees: Diagnose why disk-backed 2D R-trees choke when millions of drivers update coordinates every 4 seconds.

R-trees excel at static POI data (restaurants, parks) where bounding boxes rarely move. When 4 million active drivers emit GPS pings every 4 seconds (1M writes/sec), constantly splitting and rebalancing R-tree bounding boxes causes massive write amplification and lock contention.

FOUNDATION

Static POI vs. Moving-Object Spatial Load

1.1M+ Writes/Sec: Driver locations are ephemeral—only the latest cell matters.

2.In-Memory Grid: Discretizing to 64-bit cell IDs turns updates into O(1) hash-map swaps.

High-frequency driver GPS streams require O(1) in-memory cell updates rather than disk R-trees.

Geohash Prefix Grids & Dynamic Quadtrees.

Why this matters: Compare fixed-precision Geohash strings against density-adaptive Quadtree subdivisions.

Geohash interleaves latitude and longitude bits into a base-32 string (e.g., 9q8yyk).

Longer shared prefixes imply proximity, except across Z-order seams (e.g., 0111 vs 1000). Quadtrees recursively split dense cells into 4 quadrants until each leaf holds <= K drivers, adapting to Manhattan vs.

FOUNDATION

Geohash vs. Quadtree Trade-Offs

1.9-Cell Rule: Always query the center Geohash plus its 8 neighbors to avoid boundary misses.

2.Density Skew: Quadtrees shine for static density skew but add pointer-chasing overhead.

Both Geohash and Quadtrees use squares that suffer from unequal corner distances and boundary seams.

Uber H3 Hexagonal Grid & Uniform 6-Neighbor Geometry.

Why this matters: Eliminate diagonal distortion using H3's icosahedral hexagonal tiling.

Think of Uber H3 Hexagonal Grid & Uniform 6-Neighbor Geometry as the intuition layer for Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3 Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees: Eliminate diagonal distortion using H3's icosahedral hexagonal tiling.

In a square grid, edge neighbors are distance d away while diagonal corners are 1.414 * d away. Hexagons are the highest-symmetry polygon that tiles a plane with 100% uniform center-to-center distance to all 6 neighbors, making radius queries and spatial smoothing (surge pricing) isotropic.

INTERMEDIATE

H3 Equidistant 6-Neighbor Ring

H3 hexagons give every cell 6 equidistant edge neighbors packed into a single 64-bit integer.

Hierarchical Aperture-7 Resolution & Parent-Child Rollups.

Why this matters: Aggregate fine-grained driver counts (Res 9) into city-level surge/shard regions (Res 5-7).

H3 defines 16 resolutions (Res 0 ~4.2M km² down to Res 15 ~0.9 m²).

Each parent hexagon approximately contains 7 child hexagons (aperture 7).

INTERMEDIATE

Aperture-7 Multi-Resolution Hierarchy

1.Bitwise Rollup: Computing `cell_to_parent` is pure bit manipulation on the 64-bit H3 ID.

2.Multi-Use Index: One GPS ping powers dispatch, surge, and heatmaps simultaneously.

H3's aperture-7 hierarchy turns 64-bit bitmasks into instant multi-scale aggregations.

Progressive k-Ring Lookup & Road-Network ETA Filtering.

Why this matters: Find the fastest-arriving driver—not just the closest straight-line GPS dot.

Think of Progressive k-Ring Lookup & Road-Network ETA Filtering as the intuition layer for Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3 Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees: Find the fastest-arriving driver—not just the closest straight-line GPS dot.

A driver 200 meters away across a river or divided highway might have a 12-minute ETA, while a driver 600 meters away on the same avenue has a 2-minute ETA. Dispatch runs grid_disk(rider_h3, k=2) in memory to pull ~20–50 candidate drivers, then scores them via a pre-computed road-network ETA Matrix.

INTERMEDIATE

k-Ring Candidate Pruning + ETA

1.Progressive Expansion: Start with k=1 in dense downtowns; expand to k=3 in suburbs.

2.Haversine Trap: Straight-line distance fails across rivers, highways, and airport loops.

H3 k-ring prunes millions of drivers down to ~50 candidates for accurate road-network ETA ranking.

City-Region Sharding & Airport Hotspot Mitigation.

Why this matters: Handle 3,000 drivers idling in a single airport Res 7 cell without hot-shard memory saturation.

Think of City-Region Sharding & Airport Hotspot Mitigation as the intuition layer for Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3 Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees: Handle 3,000 drivers idling in a single airport Res 7 cell without hot-shard memory saturation.

Sharding purely by fine-grained H3 cells creates hot shards at JFK/LAX airports where thousands of drivers cluster in one staging lot. Partitioning by city/metro region (with consistent hashing across metro sub-shards) and capping per-cell candidate sampling (RESERVOIR_SAMPLE(50)) keeps airport lookups O(1).

ADVANCED

Airport Hotspot Sampling

Bounding per-cell candidate sets protects downstream ETA services at dense airport hotspots.

In-Memory Driver State, Cell Transitions & Stale-Ping Expiry.

Why this matters: Evict disconnected drivers automatically and update cell memberships atomically when a car crosses a hex edge.

Think of In-Memory Driver State, Cell Transitions & Stale-Ping Expiry as the intuition layer for Geospatial Indexing: Uber H3 Hexagons, Geohash & Quadtrees: Evict disconnected drivers automatically and update cell memberships atomically when a car crosses a hex edge.

When a driver drives from cell_A to cell_B, the index must remove driver_id from cell_A and insert into cell_B atomically so they aren't double-counted or lost. Storing a driver -> {cell_id, last_ping_ms} primary map alongside cell_id -> Set[driver_id] with a 15-second TTL sweeper ensures dead-app drivers vanish promptly.

ADVANCED

Atomic Cell Transition & TTL

Dual in-memory maps with timestamp guards keep driver cell transitions atomic and fresh.
