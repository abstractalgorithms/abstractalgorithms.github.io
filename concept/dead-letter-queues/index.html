Dead Letter Queues

Dead Letter Queues: practical patterns, trade-offs, and failure modes for production engineering teams.

Orientation & Intuition

Dead Letter Queues is a foundational topic in Distributed Systems & Storage. Engineers use it to make predictable trade-offs under load, failure, and evolving requirements.

Key takeaway Adopt Dead Letter Queues when its operational assumptions match your scale, consistency needs, and team skills.

Core Architecture & Trade-offs

Dead Letter Queues influences reliability, cost, and delivery speed. Misapplying it creates outages, data bugs, or unmaintainable systems.

Key Architecture Invariants

  • State invariants and failure modes before standardizing on Dead Letter Queues
  • Pair design decisions with metrics, alerts, and runbooks
  • Document rollback paths and degraded modes explicitly

When to Use

  • Production systems where Dead Letter Queues addresses a recurring bottleneck or risk
  • Teams that can instrument, operate, and iterate on the chosen approach

When NOT to Use

  • Early prototypes with negligible traffic and simple correctness needs
  • When a simpler primitive already meets requirements with less operational burden
