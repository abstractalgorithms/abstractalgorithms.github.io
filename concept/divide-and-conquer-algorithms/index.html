Divide & Conquer Algorithms

Decompose problems into independent subproblems, solve recursively, and combine results for merge sort, quickselect, and parallel speedups.

Orientation & Intuition

Divide & conquer splits input, solves subproblems recursively, and merges partial answers—classic examples include merge sort and binary search on answer space.

Key takeaway Look for independent subranges or subtrees; combine steps must be efficient or parallelism gains disappear.

Core Architecture & Trade-offs

It is the backbone of efficient sorting, closest-pair geometry, and many parallel algorithms.

Key Architecture Invariants

  • Recurrence relations describe cost
  • Merge step often dominates
  • Master theorem guides complexity

When to Use

  • Sorted subarray problems
  • Recursive tree problems
  • Parallel map-reduce style workloads

When NOT to Use

  • Heavy overlapping subproblems without memoization (use DP instead)
  • Tiny inputs where recursion overhead dominates

Deep Dive & Production Considerations

Pattern

Split, conquer, combine. Analyze with recurrences and watch the combine step.
