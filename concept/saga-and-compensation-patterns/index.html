Saga & Compensation Patterns

Coordinate long-lived business transactions with compensating actions instead of distributed two-phase commit locks.

Orientation & Intuition

A saga breaks a global transaction into local transactions with compensations; orchestration centralizes flow while choreography uses events.

Key takeaway Model forward steps and compensations explicitly—partial failure is normal, not exceptional.

Core Architecture & Trade-offs

Microservices cannot hold cross-service locks; sagas are how checkout, travel booking, and billing stay coherent.

Key Architecture Invariants

  • Compensations are business operations, not rollbacks
  • Orchestrator vs choreography trade-offs
  • Idempotency required on every step

When to Use

  • Multi-service business workflows
  • Long-running processes
  • When 2PC latency/availability is unacceptable

When NOT to Use

  • Single database ACID suffices
  • Compensations are impossible for the domain

Deep Dive & Production Considerations

Sagas

Prefer compensating transactions over blocking 2PC across services.
