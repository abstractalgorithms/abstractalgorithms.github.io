What is Reinforcement Learning.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) models sequential decision-making as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) tuple (S,A,P,R,γ)(S, A, P, R, \gamma)(S,A,P,R,γ). STAGE 02 · WHY WE NEED ITL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why we need RL (Delayed Feedback & Reward Hacking).

In adaptive bitrate streaming, robotics, autoscaling, and LLM alignment (RLHF), every action alters future states—making single-step supervised labels unavailable. However, RL introduces unique failure modes: Reward Hacking (the agent farms shaping bonuses without completing the task), Deadly Triad Divergence (function approximation + bootstrapping + off-policy updates spiraling QQQ to infinity), and Policy Collapse.

Always Benchmark a Simple Baseline First

Compare against a random policy, greedy heuristic, or PID controller before investing weeks in neural RL.

Normalize Observations and Rewards

Unnormalized sensor scales spanning orders of magnitude trigger exploding gradients and unstable critic predictions.

STAGE 03 · WHERE IT IS USEDL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Where RL fits (and where Bandits or Supervised Learning win).

Confirm that actions genuinely alter future states and that a fast, faithful simulator exists before choosing RL.

ChoiceWhat it buysWhat it costs
Good Candidate: Sequential Control & RLHF AlignmentOptimizes long-horizon trade-offs (energy vs. cooling, bitrate vs. rebuffer, LLM helpfulness) where per-step labels do not exist.Requires millions of simulator episodes or careful KL-constrained offline preference tuning.
Avoid: Single-Step Independent DecisionsUse **Contextual Bandits** (LinUCB/Thompson Sampling) for ad ranking or **XGBoost** when labeled ground truth exists.Full MDPs add massive variance and sample inefficiency when actions do not affect future states.
Avoid: High-Risk Production Without a SimulatorUse convex optimization (LP/MILP) or deterministic PID controllers when exploratory errors cost real money or safety.Live online exploration without a simulator or safety shield causes outages.

STAGE 04 · HOW IT IS MANAGEDL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

How PPO Clipping, Diagnostics & Safety Shields are managed.

Constrain policy updates with PPO's clipped surrogate objective (clip=0.2), monitor critic explained variance (>0) and policy entropy (avoid premature collapse), evaluate on held-out seeds, and deploy behind shadow mode + deterministic safety shields.

import torch

def ppo_clipped_loss(log_probs_new: torch.Tensor, log_probs_old: torch.Tensor,
                     advantages: torch.Tensor, clip_eps: float = 0.2) -> torch.Tensor:
    """PPO clipped surrogate objective prevents destructive policy updates."""
    ratio = torch.exp(log_probs_new - log_probs_old)
    surr1 = ratio * advantages
    surr2 = torch.clamp(ratio, 1.0 - clip_eps, 1.0 + clip_eps) * advantages
    return -torch.min(surr1, surr2).mean()

Alternatives: On-Policy PPO vs. Off-Policy SAC/DQN vs. Contextual Bandits.

Balance sample efficiency, training stability, and action-space structure.

ChoiceWhat it buysWhat it costs
On-Policy PPOStable, easy to tune, and scales cleanly across vectorized simulator workers.Discards trajectories after each update—needs 10x–100x more samples than off-policy replay.
Off-Policy DQN / SAC (Replay Buffer)High sample efficiency by reusing past transitions; clipped double-Q stabilizes value estimates.Higher hyperparameter sensitivity and deadly-triad divergence risk if untuned.
Contextual Bandits (LinUCB)Very high sample efficiency and low tuning overhead for single-step recommendations.Cannot plan across multi-step delayed rewards.
