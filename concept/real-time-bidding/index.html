OpenRTB Auction Mechanics & 100ms Budget.

Why this matters: Understand the end-to-end OpenRTB protocol flow and strict timeout penalties.

Think of OpenRTB Auction Mechanics & 100ms Budget as the intuition layer for Real-Time Bidding (RTB), CTR Calibration & Bid Shading: Understand the end-to-end OpenRTB protocol flow and strict timeout penalties.

When a user loads a webpage, the SSP/Ad Exchange broadcasts an OpenRTB JSON/Protobuf BidRequest with tmax=100ms. Any DSP response arriving after 100ms is silently dropped (0% win rate), making tail latency directly equal to lost revenue.

OpenRTB Protocol

OpenRTB 100ms Round-Trip Timeline

1.Zero-Grace Cutoff: Responses at 101ms earn zero impressions regardless of bid price.

2.Edge Colocation: Deploying bidder PoPs near Ad Exchange regions saves 20-40ms RTT.

Regional edge colocation and strict internal timeouts keep DSPs inside the 100ms OpenRTB window.

foundation

Expected Value & Negative Downsampling Correction.

Why this matters: Convert downsampled model outputs into true calibrated click probabilities.

Click rates are tiny (~0.5%–2%).

To speed up training, negative (non-click) impressions are downsampled by rate w (e.g., 0.1).

Probability Calibration

Negative Downsampling Log-Odds Correction

1.10x Overbid Hazard: Forgetting the w=0.1 correction inflates CPC bids by nearly 10x.

2.Isotonic Calibration: Non-parametric isotonic regression fixes non-linear neural score distortion.

Log-odds prior correction and isotonic calibration turn neural scores into true probabilities.

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Real-Time Bidding (RTB), CTR Calibration & Bid Shading without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.

First-Price Auctions & Bid Shading.

Why this matters: Maximize advertiser ROI by bidding just above the expected clearing price.

In second-price auctions, bidding true value v was optimal.

In modern first-price auctions, you pay what you bid.

Auction Theory

First-Price Bid Shading Optimization

1.Floor Enforcement: Never shade below the publisher's hard bidfloor or auction fails.

2.Surplus Gain: Balancing win-rate drop against CPM savings boosts net conversions per dollar.

Bid shading maximizes advertiser surplus by predicting the minimum winning bid in first-price auctions.

Why this matters: Spread daily campaign budgets smoothly across 24 hours without premature exhaustion.

Without pacing, a $10K daily campaign can burn out in 15 minutes of cheap midnight traffic.

A probabilistic PID controller adjusts each campaign's participation rate p_pace(t) every 10 seconds based on target vs.

Spend Control

PID Probabilistic Budget Pacer

1.Probabilistic vs Hard Cap: Probabilistic pacing preserves high-CTR inventory later in the day.

2.10s Sync Lag: Local bidder caches sync spend from Redis every 10s to avoid per-bid locks.

A distributed PID controller paces campaign spend smoothly across peak and off-peak hours.

Delayed Attribution & Streaming Joiners.

Why this matters: Join bid requests, win notices, impressions, and delayed clicks in Flink.

Think of Delayed Attribution & Streaming Joiners as the intuition layer for Real-Time Bidding (RTB), CTR Calibration & Bid Shading: Join bid requests, win notices, impressions, and delayed clicks in Flink.

A bid request occurs at t=0, win notice at t=50ms, impression pixel at t=500ms, and click at t=10s–24h. Flink maintains a RocksDB interval-join keyed by auction_id across a 2-hour window, emitting negative samples on timeout and positive samples on click.

Attribution

Flink Multi-Event Auction Attribution Joiner

1.Zero Leakage: Features are frozen at bid time (t=0), never re-read at click time.

2.Timer Expiry: Unclicked impressions emit label=0 once the attribution timer expires.

Flink interval joins correlate asynchronous bid, impression, and click streams by auction_id.

FTRL-Proximal Online Learning.

Why this matters: Update sparse ad-ID and publisher-ID weights in real time within minutes.

Think of FTRL-Proximal Online Learning as the intuition layer for Real-Time Bidding (RTB), CTR Calibration & Bid Shading: Update sparse ad-ID and publisher-ID weights in real time within minutes.

New ad creatives launch constantly and need rapid CTR adaptation. FTRL-Proximal (Follow-The-Regularized-Leader) with L1+L2 regularization updates sparse embedding tables online via Parameter Servers, driving irrelevant feature weights to exact zero for compact memory.

Online Learning

FTRL-Proximal Sparse Weight Updates

1.Exact L1 Sparsity: FTRL zeros out noisy cross-features, keeping sparse tables under 10 GB.

2.60s Freshness: New ad creatives converge to accurate pCTR within minutes of launch.

FTRL-Proximal delivers fast online convergence while keeping sparse feature tables compact.

Click Fraud & Invalid Traffic (IVT) Filtering.

Why this matters: Prevent bots and click-farms from draining advertiser budgets and poisoning CTR models.

Think of Click Fraud & Invalid Traffic (IVT) Filtering as the intuition layer for Real-Time Bidding (RTB), CTR Calibration & Bid Shading: Prevent bots and click-farms from draining advertiser budgets and poisoning CTR models.

Botnets generate fake clicks that distort both billing and online learning gradients. A streaming IVT detector flags headless browsers, datacenter ASN bursts, and sub-100ms click-after-impression anomalies before clicks enter billing or FTRL training.

Fraud Defense

Pre-Billing & Pre-Training IVT Gate

1.Model Poisoning Guard: Filtering IVT before FTRL prevents models from learning to target bots.

2.Zero-Cost Drop: Invalid clicks are excluded from advertiser billing automatically.

Scrubbing duplicate and bot clicks before attribution protects both billing and model weights.
