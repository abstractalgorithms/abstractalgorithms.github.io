STAGE 01 · WHAT IT ISL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

What is DNS & Traffic Routing.

Why this matters: The mechanism: understand how DNS & Traffic Routing works from first principles.

When a client resolves api.example.com, the Stub Resolver queries a Recursive Resolver (1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8), which walks Root (.) -> TLD (.com) -> Authoritative Nameserver over UDP/53 (EDNS0 up to 1232–4096 bytes; TCP fallback; or encrypted DoT/853 & DoH/443) and caches the result for TTL seconds.

Modern authoritative DNS evaluates dynamic policies—Latency/GeoDNS (via EDNS Client Subnet /24), Health Checks, and Weighted Routing—while Anycast BGP announces one identical IP across 200+ edge POPs for sub-second BGP failover.

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement DNS & Traffic Routing without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.

Regional Failover with Route 53 Health Checks

When us-east-1 failed 3 consecutive 30s /healthz checks (90s), Route 53 stopped returning the US ALB IP and routed new queries to eu-west-1 while existing clients drained over 1–3 minutes.

Anycast Edge + Origin Shielding (<5s Failover)

Putting api.example.com behind Anycast BGP across 300+ POPs terminated TLS in <20 ms locally and failed over origins in <5s over backbone health checks without waiting for client DNS TTLs.

How to Lower TTLs, Prevent NXDOMAIN Outages & Drain Connections.

Why this matters: Operational control: configure bounds, failure handling, and safe production defaults.

Pre-Migration TTL Lowering: Lower TTL to 60s at least 24 hours before cutover (lowering at cutover doesn't help clients holding a 3600s cached record). 2. Keep SOA Minimum TTL Low (60–300s): Prevents an accidental 20-second record deletion from caching NXDOMAIN for an hour. 3.

Design Trade-Off

How to Lower TTLs, Prevent NXDOMAIN Outages & Drain Connections

Before · Unbounded Coupling
  • No backpressure on spikes
  • Implicit shared state
  • Cascading tail latency
After · Bounded & Observable
  • Explicit capacity budgets
  • Isolated failure domains
  • Predictable p99 latency
# Pre-migration DNS & JVM/gRPC readiness checks
# 1. Verify authoritative TTL & SOA negative-caching minimum before cutover
dig +nocmd api.example.com A +noall +answer
dig +nocmd example.com SOA +noall +answer

# 2. Enforce 30s DNS cache TTL in JVM services (prevents infinite pinning)
java -Dsun.net.inetaddr.ttl=30 -Dsun.net.inetaddr.negative.ttl=10 -jar app.jar
