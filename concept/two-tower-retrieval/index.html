01
foundation

Why Single-Stage Ranking Fails at Scale.

Why this matters: Understand the latency wall of evaluating O(N) cross-features over 10M items.

A heavy ranking model (e.g., DCNv2 or Transformer) takes ~0.5ms per batch of 100 items on a GPU.

Scoring 10M items per request would require 50 seconds of GPU time.

Latency Funnel

10M -> 1000 -> 50 -> 10 Multi-Stage Funnel

1.10,000x Reduction: ANN retrieval narrows 10M items to 1,000 in ~5ms.

2.Compute Budget: Heavy GPU inference runs only on the top 1,000 candidates.

Progressive filtering concentrates expensive neural compute on the most promising 0.01% of items.

02
foundation

Two-Tower Neural Architecture.

Why this matters: Decouple user and item encoders so item embeddings can be pre-computed.

The User Tower ingests user ID, recent clicks, and device context to output a 128-d L2-normalized vector u.

The Item Tower ingests title, category, and publisher to output a 128-d vector v.

Dual Encoders

Decoupled User Tower & Item Tower Embeddings

1.Offline Item Encoding: Item embeddings only recompute when an article is created or edited.

2.In-Batch Negatives: Training uses other items in the mini-batch as negatives with logQ correction.

Decoupling user and item towers enables pre-indexing millions of items in HNSW.

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Two-Tower Retrieval & Multi-Stage Ranking Funnel without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
When inference latency or GPU memory limits bind throughput, never run a heavy cross-network over millions of items; use independent User and Item towers with an ANN index to narrow 10M items to 1,000 before deep ranking.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

03
intermediate

HNSW Indexing & Multi-Source Retrieval.

Why this matters: Combine ANN semantic search with trending and collaborative filtering sources.

Think of HNSW Indexing & Multi-Source Retrieval as the intuition layer for Two-Tower Retrieval & Multi-Stage Ranking Funnel: Combine ANN semantic search with trending and collaborative filtering sources.

Two-Tower ANN struggles with breaking news that lacks click history. Production retrieval merges 3–4 parallel sources: Two-Tower HNSW (600 items), Breaking/Trending News Bucket (200 items), and Item-to-Item Collaborative Filtering (200 items), deduplicating before hydration.

Multi-Source Recall

Parallel Candidate Retrieval & Deduplication

1.Cold-Start Rescue: Trending buckets ensure brand-new articles get initial impressions.

2.Bloom Seen Filter: Per-user Bloom/Roaring filters strip already-read articles.

Parallel retrieval sources cover semantic relevance, breaking freshness, and behavioral co-clicks.

04
intermediate

Feature Hydration & Heavy Cross-Ranking.

Why this matters: Score 1,000 candidates with rich user-item cross-features on GPUs.

Think of Feature Hydration & Heavy Cross-Ranking as the intuition layer for Two-Tower Retrieval & Multi-Stage Ranking Funnel: Score 1,000 candidates with rich user-item cross-features on GPUs.

After deduplication, the ranker hydrates 100+ features per candidate from Redis/Aerospike and runs a multi-task DeepFM or DCNv2 model predicting P(click), P(dwell > 30s), and P(share). Multi-task heads prevent clickbait from dominating.

Multi-Task Ranking

Feature Hydration & Multi-Task DeepFM Ranker

1.Clickbait Suppression: Weighting pDwell higher than pClick rewards genuinely engaging reads.

2.FP16 Batching: Scoring 1,000 candidates as one GPU batch maximizes TensorCore utilization.

Multi-task ranking balances click probability against dwell time and negative feedback.

05
intermediate

MMR Diversity & Freshness Re-Ranking.

Why this matters: Prevent filter bubbles and repetitive topics in the final top-10 feed.

Think of MMR Diversity & Freshness Re-Ranking as the intuition layer for Two-Tower Retrieval & Multi-Stage Ranking Funnel: Prevent filter bubbles and repetitive topics in the final top-10 feed.

Pointwise rankers often fill all 10 slots with the same breaking story. Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) greedily selects items that maximize relevance minus maximum cosine similarity to already-selected items, while enforcing publisher diversity caps.

Listwise Re-Ranking

MMR Diversity & Publisher Cap Re-Ranker

1.Filter Bubble Guard: MMR penalizes near-duplicate articles on the same topic.

2.Exploration Slot: Reserving slot #4 for Thompson Sampling explores fresh items.

Listwise MMR re-ranking transforms pointwise scores into a diverse, engaging feed.

06
advanced

LogQ Sampling Bias & Position Debiasing.

Why this matters: Correct popularity bias in Two-Tower training and position bias in rankers.

In-batch negative sampling over-penalizes popular items because they appear frequently as negatives.

Subtracting log(Q(item)) from logits (LogQ correction) restores unbiased relevance.

Debiasing

LogQ & Position-Tower Debiasing

1.Tail Item Recall: LogQ correction prevents popular items from being unfairly suppressed.

2.Position Factoring: Separating position bias lets the ranker learn true relevance.

LogQ and position-tower debiasing prevent feedback loops that starve niche content.

07
advanced

Real-Time Online Embedding Updates.

Why this matters: Adapt user interests within seconds of a click or skip.

Think of Real-Time Online Embedding Updates as the intuition layer for Two-Tower Retrieval & Multi-Stage Ranking Funnel: Adapt user interests within seconds of a click or skip.

Batch-trained user embeddings go stale during a single browsing session. A Kafka-to-Flink session aggregator updates a user's short-term interest vector in Redis within 2 seconds of each click, blending it with the long-term User Tower embedding.

Session Adaptation

2-Second Streaming User Vector Update

1.EMA Blending: Exponential moving average shifts focus without forgetting long-term tastes.

2.Re-Normalization: Always L2-normalize after blending so dot-product scales remain valid.

Streaming EMA updates adapt user embeddings within 2 seconds of live clicks.
