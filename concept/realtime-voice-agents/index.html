The idea to keep with you

When batching and tail latency compete for accelerator cycles, achieving natural conversational cadence (<300ms mouth-to-ear latency) requires overlapping ASR, LLM decoding, and neural TTS vocoding over UDP/WebRTC while propagating barge-in abort signals across every stage within 150ms.

TL;DR30-second simplified summary
▾

Transporting 20ms Opus frames over UDP/WebRTC eliminates TCP head-of-line blocking, while an adaptive jitter buffer feeds clean 16kHz PCM to Silero VAD and a semantic turn-detection model. Streaming Conformer ASR emits stabilized prefixes that prefill the LLM's PagedAttention KV-cache before end-of-turn fires.

On egress, a clause-level chunker flushes the first 8 tokens to a flow-matching TTS vocoder for ~70ms Time-to-First-Audio, then widens to 24-token spans for smooth prosody. On barge-in, the orchestrator flushes outbound SFU queues, cancels GPU inference, and trims unplayed assistant tokens using playout timestamps.

Full walkthrough · 7 stages
01
foundation

Full-Duplex WebRTC Media Transport & Jitter Buffering.

Why this matters: Eliminate TCP head-of-line blocking by transporting 20ms Opus audio and sampled video frames over UDP/WebRTC.

HTTP/REST and even WebSockets suffer from TCP head-of-line (HoL) blocking: a single dropped packet stalls the entire byte stream while TCP retransmits, spiking audio latency past 500ms on cellular networks.

WebRTC uses SRTP over UDP with Opus frames encoded at 20ms intervals (48 kHz, ~32 kbps) and Forward Error Correction (FEC) so isolated packet loss is concealed without stalling.

STAGE 1 · TRANSPORT

WebRTC UDP Media Ingress & Jitter Smoothing

1.Why UDP Wins: Dropping one stale 20ms Opus frame beats stalling 400ms for a TCP retransmission.

2.Vision Throttling: Sample video at 1-2 FPS or on motion delta to keep vision tokens under 15% of GPU budget.

WebRTC SFUs terminate DTLS-SRTP at the edge and feed an adaptive jitter buffer that emits jitter-free 16kHz PCM frames.

02
foundation

Voice Activity Detection (VAD) & Semantic Turn Detection.

Why this matters: Distinguish natural mid-sentence pauses from true end-of-turn intent without cutting off the user.

Naive energy-threshold VAD cuts users off whenever they pause for 300ms to breathe or think ('um...').

Production agents pair a lightweight neural frame classifier (Silero VAD, <1ms per 30ms frame on CPU) with a prosody- and text-aware turn-detection model (e.g., Smart-Turn / tiny transformer over the trailing 4 seconds of audio + partial ASR).

STAGE 2 · TURN DETECTION

Hybrid Acoustic + Semantic Turn State Machine

Combining sub-millisecond Silero VAD with a semantic turn classifier cuts premature interruptions by over 60%.

Problem

Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision

Trade-Offs

03
intermediate

Streaming ASR & Speculative KV-Cache Prefill.

Why this matters: Overlap speech recognition with incremental LLM KV-cache prefill before the user finishes speaking.

Waiting for end-of-turn before running Whisper or LLM prefill wastes 150–300ms.

Instead, streaming Conformer/RNN-T ASR or chunked Whisper with local agreement emits stabilized word prefixes every 100ms. As each stabilized word chunk arrives, the LLM serving engine asynchronously prefills the prompt's KV-cache blocks in GPU HBM without generating output tokens yet.

STAGE 3 · INGRESS AI

Streaming ASR with Speculative KV-Cache Prefill

1.Rollback on Revision: If ASR revises a trailing word, only the last 1-2 KV blocks are invalidated.

2.TTFT Savings: Speculative prefill hides 70%+ of prompt-processing latency behind natural speech.

Streaming ASR feeds stabilized token prefixes into PagedAttention KV-cache blocks while the user is still speaking.

04
intermediate

Clause-Level Token-to-Phoneme Chunking & Streaming TTS.

Why this matters: Start synthesizing natural-prosody audio on the first 6–10 LLM tokens without waiting for the full response.

Neural TTS vocoders need enough lookahead context to get pitch, stress, and coarticulation right—streaming single tokens produces choppy, robotic speech.

A clause-level chunker buffers LLM tokens until it hits a prosodic boundary (comma, period, or 8–12 tokens on the first chunk for low Time-to-First-Audio) and immediately dispatches the text span to a streaming TTS model (e.g., flow-matching mel-spectrogram + HiFi-GAN/Vocos).

STAGE 4 · EGRESS AI

Prosody-Aware Clause Chunker & Neural Vocoder

1.First-Chunk Trade-off: Flushing at 8 tokens cuts TTFA to ~70ms; later chunks use 24 tokens for smooth prosody.

2.Pacer Discipline: Opus frames must be paced at 20ms intervals; bursting frames overflows client jitter buffers.

An adaptive clause chunker balances 70ms Time-to-First-Audio on the opening span with 24-token prosody stability thereafter.

05
intermediate

Full-Duplex Barge-In Cancellation & State Reconciliation.

Why this matters: Abort in-flight LLM/TTS generation within 150ms when the user interrupts and reconcile the unplayed transcript.

Think of Full-Duplex Barge-In Cancellation & State Reconciliation as the intuition layer for Real-Time Voice & Multimodal AI Streaming: Abort in-flight LLM/TTS generation within 150ms when the user interrupts and reconcile the unplayed transcript.

When a user says 'Wait, stop—' while the assistant is mid-sentence, three things must happen atomically within 150ms: (1) the SFU/client flushes its queued outbound Opus frames so audio stops immediately; (2) an asyncio.Event / gRPC cancellation signal aborts both the TTS vocoder and the LLM KV-cache decode loop; and (3) the session manager computes played_chars from the actual audio frames acknowledged by the client playout clock, truncating the assistant's turn in conversation history to only what the user actually heard. Without this truncation, the LLM assumes the user heard the unplayed suffix and gives incoherent follow-up answers.

STAGE 5 · BARGE-IN

150ms Barge-In Abort & Playout-Clock Reconciliation

1.Acoustic Echo Trap: Hardware/WebRTC AEC must suppress agent playback before barge-in VAD evaluates.

2.Context Integrity: Truncate unplayed tokens via word-level timestamps so LLM memory matches user perception.

Barge-in cancellation flushes egress audio buffers, aborts GPU decoding, and trims unplayed tokens using playout timestamps.

06
advanced

Multimodal Vision-Audio Synchronization & Native Speech-to-Speech.

Why this matters: Synchronize periodic video keyframes with audio timestamps and evaluate native omni-modal models.

In multimodal agents (e.g., live camera troubleshooting or screen-sharing copilots), video frames arrive at 1–2 FPS alongside continuous 50 FPS Opus audio.

Each frame is tagged with a monotonic RTP/NTP presentation timestamp (PTS) so the context assembler interleaves [IMG@t=12.4s] directly adjacent to the spoken words from t=12.0s..12.8s.

STAGE 6 · MULTIMODAL

Cascaded Streaming vs. Native Speech-to-Speech Omni

1.Cascaded Advantage: Easier PII redaction, deterministic tool calls, and independent ASR/TTS model swaps.

2.Omni Advantage: Preserves laughter, whisper, and emotion that text transcripts strip away.

Monotonic PTS alignment synchronizes video keyframes with speech, while cascaded vs. native omni architectures trade modularity for sub-200ms speed.

07
advanced

Latency Budget Engineering, Failure Modes & Graceful Degradation.

Why this matters: Enforce a strict <300ms voice-to-voice SLA with circuit breakers for GPU saturation and network jitter.

Natural human turn-taking averages 200–300ms.

To stay under 300ms p95, allocate a strict stage budget: Network/SFU <=40ms, VAD+ASR <=60ms, LLM TTFT <=110ms, TTS First-Byte <=70ms, Playout <=20ms.

STAGE 7 · SLA & RESILIENCE

300ms Latency Budget & Load-Shedding Circuit Breakers

1.Tail Latency Killer: A single 600ms GC pause or KV-cache eviction breaks conversational flow.

2.Bounded Retries: Never retry a timed-out audio frame; skip stale chunks and advance the playout clock.

Strict per-stage latency budgets and automatic degradation (vision throttling, smaller draft models, tool fillers) protect conversational cadence under load.
