Metrics Cardinality
Control.

Metrics cardinality is the number of unique label-value combinations (metric_name + {k=v}) tracked in a Time-Series Database; unbounded labels like user_id or request_id explode inverted indexes, exhausting TSDB RAM and crashing Prometheus/Mimir.

STAGE 01 · WHAT IT ISL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

What is Metrics Cardinality.

Why this matters: The mechanism: understand how Metrics Cardinality works from first principles.

In Prometheus, VictoriaMetrics, and Mimir, every unique combination of metric_name and label values {k=v} creates a distinct time series with its own in-memory Head chunk, inverted index entry, and 2-hour block compaction cost.

Total cardinality is the Cartesian product of active label values: 50 endpoints × 5 status_codes × 4 methods × 100 pods × 12 histogram buckets = 1,200,000 series from a single HTTP histogram metric. At ~3–5 KB per active series in the Head block, 10M series consumes 30–50 GB of RAM before any query runs.

TSDB Inverted Index

Label Cartesian Product -> Head Block Memory Explosion

1.1. Cartesian Multiplier: Adding one unbounded label (user_id or un-templated /users/:id) multiplies every bucket by N.

2.2. Churn Kills Compaction: Short-lived CronJob pod_name labels create millions of dead series in the 2-hour Head block.

3.3. Exemplars Bridge: Attach trace_id as an Exemplar on histogram buckets instead of a metric label.

Keep metric labels strictly bounded (<100 values per label) and push high-cardinality IDs to traces, logs, and Exemplars.

STAGE 02 · WHY WE NEED ITL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why Head-Block OOMs and labeldrop collisions happen.

Why this matters: The problem: see what breaks without Why Head-Block OOMs and labeldrop collisions happen under real concurrency or scale.

A single developer adding user_id or un-normalized /users/98421/orders URLs to a Prometheus counter can OOM-crash the central TSDB across an entire cluster.

Even worse, trying to 'fix' it in Prometheus with labeldrop on instance or user_id leaves multiple distinct series with identical remaining labels—causing duplicate sample / out-of-order timestamp collisions that silently drop metrics.

Design Trade-Off

Why Head-Block OOMs and labeldrop collisions happen

Before · Unbounded Coupling
  • No backpressure on spikes
  • Implicit shared state
  • Cascading tail latency
After · Bounded & Observable
  • Explicit capacity budgets
  • Isolated failure domains
  • Predictable p99 latency
Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Metrics Cardinality Control without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
When traffic spikes expose hidden coupling, never put high-cardinality identifiers (user IDs, IPs, URLs with UUIDs) in metric labels—normalize routes at the middleware, enforce scrape sample_limits, and route per-request debugging to logs, traces, and Exemplars.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

Request & State Flow

Scrape Sample Limits & Router-Template Normalization

# prometheus.yml: Central circuit breaker against cardinality bombs
scrape_configs:
  - job_name: 'payments-api'
    sample_limit: 50000      # Reject target scrape if series exceed 50k
    label_limit: 20
    label_value_length_limit: 128
    metric_relabel_configs:
      - source_labels: [__name__]
        regex: 'http_request_duration_seconds_bucket'
        action: keep
