What is Load Balancing (L4 vs L7 & Consistent Hashing).

Why this matters: The mechanism: understand how Load Balancing (L4 vs L7 & Consistent Hashing) works from first principles.

A load balancer distributes incoming connections or requests across a pool of backend servers while removing unhealthy instances from rotation:

  • L4 (Transport): routes on (src_ip, src_port, dst_ip, dst_port, proto) using IPVS/eBPF/Maglev at >10M packets/sec.
  • L7 (Application): terminates TLS/HTTP to inspect paths, headers, and gRPC frames.

STAGE 02 · WHY WE NEED ITL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why Round Robin and L4-only fail on gRPC and heterogeneous workloads.

Why this matters: The problem: see what breaks without Why Round Robin and L4-only fail on gRPC and heterogeneous workloads under real concurrency or scale.

Without load balancing, one overloaded instance or AZ outage takes down the service.

Even with a balancer, two classic traps spike p99 latency:

  • The gRPC / HTTP/2 Trap: HTTP/2 multiplexes thousands of RPCs over one long-lived TCP connection. An L4 balancer pins that single TCP connection to one backend pod forever.
L4 vs L7 gRPC Routing

Why gRPC Requires L7 or Client-Side Load Balancing

L7 proxies multiplex-unwrap HTTP/2 streams and use P2C+EWMA to steer RPCs away from degraded pods.

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Load Balancing & Consistent Hashing without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
When a single bottleneck threatens end-to-end availability, match the balancing layer and algorithm to your traffic: use L4 (Maglev/DSR) for raw packet scale, L7 (Envoy/ALB) for gRPC/HTTP routing, and Power of Two Choices (P2C) + EWMA to crush tail latency.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

STAGE 04 · HOW IT IS MANAGEDL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Power of Two Choices (P2C) + Outlier & Slow-Start Config.

Why this matters: Operational control: configure bounds, failure handling, and safe production defaults.

Think of Power of Two Choices (P2C) + Outlier & Slow-Start Config as the intuition layer for Load Balancing & Consistent Hashing: Operational control: configure bounds, failure handling, and safe production defaults.

Envoy and Linkerd use P2C + Peak EWMA: sample 2 random backends and pick the one with lower active requests/latency. Pair this with max_ejection_percent: 50 (panic threshold) and slow_start_config so newly booted pods warm caches gradually.

Design Trade-Off

Power of Two Choices (P2C) + Outlier & Slow-Start Config

Before · Unbounded Coupling
  • No backpressure on spikes
  • Implicit shared state
  • Cascading tail latency
After · Bounded & Observable
  • Explicit capacity budgets
  • Isolated failure domains
  • Predictable p99 latency

lb_policy: LEAST_REQUEST
least_request_lb_config:
  choice_count: 2
  slow_start_config:
    slow_start_window: 45s
outlier_detection:
  consecutive_5xx: 5
  interval: 10s
  base_ejection_time: 30s
  max_ejection_percent: 50  # Panic threshold prevents cascading domino ejection

ChoiceWhat it buysWhat it costs
