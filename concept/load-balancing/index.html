STAGE 01 · WHAT IT ISL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

What is Load Balancing (L4 vs L7 & Consistent Hashing).

A load balancer distributes incoming connections or requests across a pool of backend servers while removing unhealthy instances from rotation:

  • L4 (Transport): routes on (src_ip, src_port, dst_ip, dst_port, proto) using IPVS/eBPF/Maglev at >10M packets/sec.
  • L7 (Application): terminates TLS/HTTP to inspect paths, headers, and gRPC frames.
  • Consistent Hashing: maps keys (tenant_id, cache_key) onto a hash ring with virtual nodes so adding/removing a backend only remaps $1/N$ keys.

STAGE 02 · WHY WE NEED ITL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why Round Robin and L4-only fail on gRPC and heterogeneous workloads.

Without load balancing, one overloaded instance or AZ outage takes down the service. Even with a balancer, two classic traps spike p99 latency:

  • The gRPC / HTTP/2 Trap: HTTP/2 multiplexes thousands of RPCs over one long-lived TCP connection. An L4 balancer pins that single TCP connection to one backend pod forever.
  • Health-Check Herd Collapse: if one slow node fails /healthz and gets ejected, its load dumps onto the remaining nodes, knocking them out in a domino cascade.
L4 vs L7 gRPC Routing

Why gRPC Requires L7 or Client-Side Load Balancing

L7 proxies multiplex-unwrap HTTP/2 streams and use P2C+EWMA to steer RPCs away from degraded pods.

04
STAGE 04 · HOW IT IS MANAGEDL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Power of Two Choices (P2C) + Outlier & Slow-Start Config.

Envoy and Linkerd use P2C + Peak EWMA: sample 2 random backends and pick the one with lower active requests/latency. Pair this with max_ejection_percent: 50 (panic threshold) and slow_start_config so newly booted pods warm caches gradually.

# Envoy Upstream Cluster: P2C + Outlier Ejection + Slow-Start
lb_policy: LEAST_REQUEST
least_request_lb_config:
  choice_count: 2
  slow_start_config:
    slow_start_window: 45s
outlier_detection:
  consecutive_5xx: 5
  interval: 10s
  base_ejection_time: 30s
  max_ejection_percent: 50  # Panic threshold prevents cascading domino ejection

STAGE 05 · ALTERNATIVESL6 · SCALE & TRADE-OFFS

Alternatives: L4 (IPVS/Maglev) vs. L7 (Envoy/ALB) vs. Client-Side.

Choose between packet-rate scale, protocol intelligence, and zero-hop client routing.

ChoiceWhat it buysWhat it costs
L4 (IPVS / Maglev / DSR)Handles >10M PPS with near-zero CPU; Direct Server Return preserves client IP.Cannot inspect HTTP paths/headers or rebalance multiplexed HTTP/2 connections.
L7 (Envoy / ALB / NGINX)Path/header routing, gRPC stream balancing, retries, and circuit breaking.Higher CPU/memory per connection due to TLS termination and HTTP parsing.
Round Robin vs. P2C-EWMARound Robin is stateless and O(1).Round Robin ignores heterogeneous request costs (5s export vs 5ms health check).
06
STAGE 06 · PRODUCTION ARCHITECTUREL7 · STAFF / PRINCIPAL

Two-Tier Edge: L4 Anycast/Maglev -> L7 Envoy Mesh.

Hyperscale architectures combine an L4 Maglev/ECMP edge (horizontally scaling across BGP/Anycast IPs) in front of L7 Envoy gateways that terminate TLS, separate /livez from /readyz, and apply P2C + panic-threshold routing.

Separate /livez from /readyz

Never tie /readyz to downstream DB checks unless you want a DB hiccup to eject all pods simultaneously.

Slow-Start Warmup

Ramp traffic weight linearly over 30–60 s so JIT compilers and L1 caches warm up before receiving full QPS.
