What is Microservices Decomposition.

Why this matters: The mechanism: understand how Microservices Decomposition works from first principles.

Microservices decomposition partitions a system into independently deployable services aligned to Domain-Driven Design (DDD) bounded contexts (discovered via Event Storming), each owning its own private database schema.

Instead of slicing horizontally (UI, Business, DB) or by CRUD nouns (User, Address), boundaries follow transactional consistency and team ownership.

STAGE 02 · WHY WE NEED ITL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why premature decomposition creates a Distributed Monolith.

Why this matters: The problem: see what breaks without Why premature decomposition creates a Distributed Monolith under real concurrency or scale.

Poorly chosen boundaries turn nanosecond in-memory calls into 2–10 ms network hops, replace ACID transactions with Sagas, and yield composite availability drops (5 synchronous 99.9% services = $0.999^5 \approx 99.5\%$ availability).

Two anti-patterns dominate failed migrations: Distributed Monolith (services share a DB schema or deploy in lockstep) and Chatty RPC Fan-Out (30 sequential N+1 calls per request).

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Microservices Decomposition without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
If two services must update state together synchronously on every write, they belong in the same service boundary until you can decouple them with an outbox and asynchronous events.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

Design Trade-Off

Decoupling Synchronous Writes with Outbox + Retry Budgets

Before · Unbounded Coupling
  • No backpressure on spikes
  • Implicit shared state
  • Cascading tail latency
After · Bounded & Observable
  • Explicit capacity budgets
  • Isolated failure domains
  • Predictable p99 latency
