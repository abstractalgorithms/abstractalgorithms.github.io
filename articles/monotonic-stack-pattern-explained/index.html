Stage 1 — The Intuition behind Monotonic Stacks

  • To understand the core mechanics of a monotonic stack, imagine a line of people waiting to buy tickets at a theater.
  • Each person in line can see the screen unless someone taller stands in front of them.
  • If a very tall person enters the line, they block the view of everyone shorter standing behind them.
  • In computer science, this visual blocking behavior represents the Next Greater Element problem.

Monotonic Decreasing Stack: Elements are ordered from largest to smallest.
[ 10, 8, 5, 2 ]  <-- Top of Stack is 2

If 7 arrives:
1. Pop 2 (7 > 2)
2. Pop 5 (7 > 5)
3. Push 7 (7 < 8)
Resulting Stack: [ 10, 8, 7 ]

Observable outcome: In computer science, this visual blocking behavior represents the Next Greater Element problem.

Stage 2 — Mechanics: Maintaining the Monotonic Property

  • A monotonic stack is simply a standard stack with an insertion invariant: - Monotonic Increasing Stack: Elements are sorted from bottom to top in increasing order.
  • When inserting a new element $X$, we must pop all elements larger than $X$ from the top of the stack before pushing $X$.
    • Monotonic Decreasing Stack: Elements are sorted from bottom to top in decreasing order.
  • When inserting $X$, we must pop all elements smaller than $X$ before pushing $X$.
