Fixing Redis Fork
Copy-on-Write Memory Bloat.

When Linux Transparent Huge Pages (2 MB) is enabled, a 64-byte write during Redis BGSAVE fork() copies an entire 2 MB page (512x write amplification), doubling RSS from 24 GB to 48 GB in 90 seconds and triggering the OOM killer.

The idea to keep with you

When Redis runs `BGSAVE` or `BGREWRITEAOF`, `fork()` shares memory pages with the child via Copy-on-Write (CoW), disable Transparent Huge Pages (`echo never > /sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled`), cap Redis `maxmemory` at <= 55% of host RAM, monitor `rdb_last_cow_size`, and gate BGSAVE during heavy write bursts.

STAGE 02 · IMPACTL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

RSS doubling from 24 GB to 48 GB and Linux OOM kills.

Why this matters: The problem: see what breaks without RSS doubling from 24 GB to 48 GB and Linux OOM kills under real concurrency or scale.

On a 32 GB host holding a 24 GB dataset (75% RAM), BGSAVE with THP enabled causes two catastrophic failures:

  1. OOM Kill in 70 Seconds: Random writes dirty 2 MB hugepages until RSS climbs past 32 GB (toward 48 GB), triggering the Linux OOM killer.
  2. 20–50 ms fork() Stalls: Allocating and compacting hugepage tables (khugepaged) stalls the main aeMain thread.

Disabling 2 MB THP drops CoW granularity back to 4 KB pages, cutting worst-case CoW bloat by up to 512x.

Think of Why setting maxmemory to 85% or transient sysfs edits fail as the intuition layer for Fixing Redis Fork Copy-on-Write Memory Bloat: The problem: see what breaks without Why setting maxmemory to 85% or transient sysfs edits fail under real concurrency or scale.

Running a fork()-enabled Redis primary at 75%–85% of host RAM leaves less than 25% headroom for normal 4 KB CoW overhead, replica output buffers, and jemalloc fragmentation. Moreover, running echo never > /sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled without persisting it in systemd/GRUB reverts silently on the next host reboot.

04
STAGE 04 · ENTERPRISE SOLUTIONL6 · SCALE & TRADE-OFFS

Enterprise solution: Disable THP, Cap maxmemory <= 55%, and Offload BGSAVE.

Why this matters: Operational control: configure bounds, failure handling, and safe production defaults.

Guarantee used_memory + CoW_overhead <= 85% of 32 GB RAM during 90-second snapshots:

  • Permanently Disable THP: Set transparent_hugepage=never in kernel boot args / systemd unit (enabled and defrag), cutting CoW page size from 2 MB to 4 KB.
  • Cap maxmemory <= 55% (~17.6 GB): Reserves ~14.4 GB for 5.4 GB worst-case 4 KB CoW, replication buffers, and OS page cache.
  • Offload BGSAVE to a Dedicated Replica: Disable save on the primary and run RDB/AOF snapshots on a read replica, monitoring rdb_last_cow_size.
Safe Persistence Topology

THP=never (4 KB CoW) + Replica Snapshot Offload

1.THP = never: Persistently disable 2 MB Transparent Huge Pages across reboots so CoW copies 4 KB pages.

2.55% RAM Ceiling: Never exceed 55% maxmemory on any node that calls fork() for RDB, AOF, or full resync.

3.Monitor rdb_last_cow_size: Alert if CoW size approaches 25% of host RAM; offload scheduled snapshots to a replica.

4 KB CoW pages + 55% maxmemory cap + replica snapshot offload keep total RSS safely under 85% of host RAM.

05
STAGE 05 · SYSTEM DESIGN APPLICATIONSL7 · STAFF / PRINCIPAL

Where fork() CoW sizing governs stateful infrastructure.

Why this matters: Scope boundary: identify where this pattern fits best and where simpler or async alternatives win.

OS page-table and CoW mechanics apply to all fork-based persistence engines:

  • Production Redis / Valkey Clusters: Standardize THP=never, vm.overcommit_memory=1, and 50–55% memory caps.
  • Managed Cache Services (ElastiCache / Memorystore): Reserve reserved-memory-percent (25–45%) specifically for BGSAVE and failover syncs.
  • High-Write AOF Compaction: Gate BGREWRITEAOF during peak traffic windows.
