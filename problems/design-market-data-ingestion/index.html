Three-Way Disruptor Fan-Out Topology.

Why this matters: Feed Strategy, Durable Wire Log, and Time-Series DB from one normalized stream.

Think of Three-Way Disruptor Fan-Out Topology as the intuition layer for Design a High-Frequency Stock Market Data Ingestion System (10M+ msgs/sec): Feed Strategy, Durable Wire Log, and Time-Series DB from one normalized stream.

Decoding ITCH/FIX once in the Feed Handler and writing to a Disruptor Ring Buffer allows three independent consumers to read the same memory-mapped slots: (1) Strategy Execution Engine, (2) Aeron/Chronicle Wire Log, and (3) kdb+/ClickHouse Batch Loader.

Fan-Out Architecture

Disruptor 3-Consumer Fan-Out

1.Decode Once: Normalizing binary exchange formats once saves 3x CPU across consumers.

2.Zero-Copy Mmap: Chronicle/Aeron appends ticks to page-cache mmap files in <200ns.

Single-producer Disruptor fan-out eliminates duplicate parsing and network hops.

Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
When traffic spikes expose hidden coupling, use a Disruptor Ring Buffer to fan out arbitrated, PTP-stamped ticks to three isolated consumers: Strategy Engine (ultra-low latency), Aeron Wire Log (durable), and kdb+/ClickHouse (analytics).
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

kdb+ / ClickHouse Time-Series Partitioning & Compression.

Why this matters: Store 100B+ daily ticks efficiently for millisecond quant backtests.

Think of kdb+ / ClickHouse Time-Series Partitioning & Compression as the intuition layer for Design a High-Frequency Stock Market Data Ingestion System (10M+ msgs/sec): Store 100B+ daily ticks efficiently for millisecond quant backtests.

Storing 10M msgs/sec for a 6.5-hour trading day produces ~50B–100B rows (~3 TB raw). Partitioning ClickHouse/kdb+ by (trade_date, symbol_bucket) and sorting by (symbol, exchange_ts_ns) with Delta+ZSTD codec achieves 8:1 compression and sub-second symbol range scans.

Columnar Delta+ZSTD Tick Compression

1.Delta Encoding: Consecutive stock prices and timestamps change by tiny deltas, compressing 8x.

2.Large Batches: Flushing 100K rows/insert avoids ClickHouse 'Too many parts' errors.

Sorted MergeTree tables with Delta+ZSTD codecs shrink daily terabytes of ticks by 8x.

Symbol Sharding & NUMA-Aware Core Pinning.

Why this matters: Scale across 64-core servers without cross-socket QPI/UPI memory penalties.

Think of Symbol Sharding & NUMA-Aware Core Pinning as the intuition layer for Design a High-Frequency Stock Market Data Ingestion System (10M+ msgs/sec): Scale across 64-core servers without cross-socket QPI/UPI memory penalties.

Dual-socket servers suffer ~40ns extra latency when a thread on Socket 0 reads memory allocated on Socket 1. We shard exchange multicast groups by symbol range across NUMA nodes, pinning the NIC IRQ/poll thread, Disruptor ring, and Strategy thread onto the same physical NUMA socket.

Hardware Affinity

NUMA-Local NIC -> Ring -> Strategy Pinning

1.Zero Cross-Socket: Keeping NIC, ring buffer, and strategy on one NUMA node cuts jitter by 40%.

2.Multicast Sharding: Exchanges split symbols across 4-8 multicast channels for easy horizontal sharding.

NUMA-local sharding eliminates cross-socket interconnect latency on high-core servers.

Market-Open Burst Cushioning & Drop Alerts.

Why this matters: Detect silent NIC ring drops or micro-bursts before strategies trade on stale books.

Think of Market-Open Burst Cushioning & Drop Alerts as the intuition layer for Design a High-Frequency Stock Market Data Ingestion System (10M+ msgs/sec): Detect silent NIC ring drops or micro-bursts before strategies trade on stale books.

If a 10M/s micro-burst overflows the NIC RX ring, the hardware rx_dropped counter increments. A dedicated Telemetry Watcher polls NIC ethtool counters every 10ms; any drop or sequence gap >5ms automatically halts quoting on affected symbols to prevent trading on stale prices.

Safety Kill-Switch

NIC Drop Counter & Stale-Book Kill-Switch

1.Never Trade Stale: Quoting on a book missing even 1 cancel message can cause instant adverse fills.

2.Auto-Resume: Re-enable quoting only after a full TCP snapshot verifies sequence parity.

Hardware NIC drop monitoring and 5ms gap timers trigger instant quote cancellation on stale books.
