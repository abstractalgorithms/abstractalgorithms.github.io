The idea to keep with you

When a single bottleneck threatens end-to-end availability, never place a slow I/O sink on the primary Disruptor gating barrier—decouple it with an async spillover buffer and explicit drop/coalesce policy.

TL;DR30-second simplified summary
▾

At 2M msg/s on a 65K ring, a 33 ms AnalyticsSink pause stalls all consumers via min(gatingSequences), while removing the gate corrupts mutable slots. Decouple AnalyticsSink with a fast non-blocking Tap + bounded spillover queue, raise the primary ring to 2^20 slots, and switch shared-core consumers to Yielding/LiteBlocking.

Before you begin · Building blocksAlready know these? Jump to Stage 01 ↓
LMAX Disruptor & Lock-Free Ring Buffers
The LMAX Disruptor replaces lock-contended queues with a pre-allocated power-of-two ring buffer, single-writer/CAS sequence claiming, and 64-byte cache-line padding to eliminate GC pauses and false sharing.
Backpressure & Flow Control
Backpressure lets a slow consumer signal an upstream producer to slow down or pause, preventing unbounded memory growth and out-of-memory crashes.
