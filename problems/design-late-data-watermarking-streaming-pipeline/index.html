The idea to keep with you

Full walkthrough · 5 stages
01
STAGE 01 · WHY IT MATTERSL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why event-time vs. processing-time breaks mobile analytics.

Mobile apps in subways or airplanes buffer telemetry locally and flush events 30 minutes (or 6 hours) after they occurred. Grouping by processing time misattributes sessions, while keeping every 5-minute window open forever in JVM heap to wait for 6-hour stragglers crashes executors with OOM over months of operation.

02
STAGE 02 · IMPACTL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Unbounded state OOMs, small-file explosions, and silent data loss.

At 40,000 events/sec across 64 Kafka partitions (600,000 events per 15 s micro-batch):

  1. JVM State Store OOM: Default HDFS-backed in-memory state stores hold all open windows in executor heap, triggering Full GC and OOM kills.
  2. Silent Outlier Loss: Dropping events older than 30 minutes silently loses 6-hour audit records unless routed to a backfill path.
  3. Small-File Explosion: Writing raw 15 s micro-batches to S3 creates thousands of tiny Parquet files/hour.
Watermark & State Eviction

30-Min Event-Time Watermark + RocksDB State + Ultra-Late DLQ

RocksDB keeps 30 minutes of active windows off-heap while ultra-late (>30m) records route to a backfill DLQ.

03
STAGE 03 · NAIVE SOLUTIONL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Naive `6-hour` watermarks or unwatermarked `groupBy`.

Setting a 6-hour watermark forces the state store to retain 40,000 × 21,600 = 864M events' worth of open window state and delays final window emissions by 6 hours. Conversely, omitting a watermark entirely leaks state until the cluster OOMs.

04
STAGE 04 · ENTERPRISE SOLUTIONL6 · SCALE & TRADE-OFFS

Enterprise solution: 30-Min Watermark + RocksDB + Side-Output DLQ.

Bound state, protect SLAs, and preserve auditability:

  • 30-Minute Watermark: withWatermark("event_time", "30 minutes") tracks max(event_time) - 30m, capturing 99%+ of subway buffering while letting RocksDB evict closed 5-minute windows.
  • Off-Heap RocksDBStateStoreProvider: Moves state out of JVM heap onto local NVMe SSDs with incremental checkpointing to S3.
  • Ultra-Late Side Output (mapGroupsWithState): Events arriving > 30m late are tagged and written to a late_events_audit Delta/Parquet table for hourly batch merge/backfill.
State Store Architecture

Off-Heap RocksDB State Store with Incremental S3 Checkpoints

RocksDB bounds executor JVM heap flat across months of 40k/sec streaming while incremental checkpoints guard recovery.

spark.conf.set(
    "spark.sql.streaming.stateStore.providerClass",
    "org.apache.spark.sql.execution.streaming.state.RocksDBStateStoreProvider"
)

windowed = (
    events_df
    .withWatermark("event_time", "30 minutes")
    .groupBy(window("event_time", "5 minutes"), "device_id")
    .agg(count("*").alias("events"), sum("bytes").alias("total_bytes"))
)
05
STAGE 05 · SYSTEM DESIGN APPLICATIONSL7 · STAFF / PRINCIPAL

Where watermarking and RocksDB state stores are used.

Event-time watermarking and off-heap RocksDB state power production streaming:

  • Apache Flink & Spark Structured Streaming: Both use RocksDB state backends to scale terabytes of keyed window state beyond JVM RAM.
  • Lakehouse Medallion Pipelines: Stream late events into Bronze/DLQ and run periodic MERGE INTO Silver tables.
