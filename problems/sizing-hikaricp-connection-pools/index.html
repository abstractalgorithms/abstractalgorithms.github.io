The idea to keep with you

Your inventory Spring Boot service (10 pods, each with `HikariCP maximumPoolSize=20`, `connectionTimeout=30000`) runs on an 8-core PostgreSQL primary. Normally `GET /items/{id}` takes 4 ms at 1,500 RPS. When an admin triggers an unindexed `GET /items/search?q=widget` query taking 12 seconds at just 25 RPS, the entire service locks up with `HikariPool-1 - Connection is not available, request timed out after 30000ms`—even for healthy `/items/{id}` lookups. Diagnose the starvation and harden the pool.

Full walkthrough · 5 stages
01
STAGE 01 · WHY IT MATTERSL4 · FUNDAMENTALS

Why Little's Law turns a 25 RPS slow query into a full outage.

Across 10 Spring Boot pods (maximumPoolSize=20 each, 200 total connections) hitting an 8-core PostgreSQL primary, GET /items/{id} normally takes 4 ms at 1,500 RPS (L = 1500 * 0.004 = 6 active connections). When an unindexed GET /items/search takes 12 s at just 25 RPS, Little's Law demands L = 25 * 12 = 300 connections—instantly starving all 200 pool slots.

02
STAGE 02 · IMPACTL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

30-second HikariPool timeouts and Tomcat thread exhaustion.

With HikariCP's default connectionTimeout=30000 (30 seconds), slow search queries trigger a two-stage cascade:

  1. Pool Starvation: All 20 connections per pod are pinned by 12 s queries (20 / 12 = 1.67 RPS/pod capacity).
  2. Tomcat Thread Pileup: Every fast 4 ms /items/{id} call blocks for 30 s waiting for a connection, exhausting all 200 Tomcat threads per pod.
Connection Pool Starvation

25 RPS Unindexed Query Starves 1,500 RPS Fast Lookups

Bounding statement_timeout, lowering connectionTimeout to 1.5s, and isolating slow search pools protect fast lookups.

03
STAGE 03 · NAIVE SOLUTIONL5 · APPLIED DESIGN

Why raising maximumPoolSize from 20 to 200 makes Postgres slower.

Inflating maximumPoolSize=200 across 10 pods opens 2,000 connections against max_connections=300 (failing outright) or thrashes an 8-core CPU with massive context-switching, lock contention, and memory bloat (~10 MB per Postgres backend). Fast, non-blocking DB cores outperform giant pools.

04
STAGE 04 · ENTERPRISE SOLUTIONL6 · SCALE & TRADE-OFFS

Enterprise solution: Little's Law Pool Sizing, Timeouts, and Bulkheads.

Protect the 8-core PostgreSQL primary and keep /items/{id} p99 < 25 ms with four controls:

  • Formula-Based Sizing: Size total active connections near (cores * 2) + effective_spindle_count (~16–20 per primary, or ~4–6 per pod across 10 pods, up to 10 with PgBouncer) instead of inflating pools.
  • Fail-Fast connectionTimeout=1500 (1.5 s): Reject callers in < 1.5 s with 503 instead of pinning Tomcat threads for 30 s.
  • DB statement_timeout=2000 + JDBC socketTimeout: Kill runaway unindexed queries on PostgreSQL at 2 s before they monopolize slots.
  • Bulkhead Pool Isolation: Route /items/search to a separate read-replica / capped DataSource (e.g., max 4 connections/pod) so search never touches the primary lookup pool.
Bulkhead & Timeout Protection

Separate Fast/Slow HikariCP Pools + Postgres statement_timeout

1.1.5s Fail-Fast: Lowering connectionTimeout from 30s to 1.5s prevents Tomcat thread pileups.

2.2s statement_timeout: PostgreSQL aborts unindexed runaway queries automatically before pools saturate.

3.Read-Replica Bulkhead: Isolates slow /search traffic onto its own small pool and read replica.

Isolating slow search endpoints and enforcing strict DB/pool timeouts keeps fast OLTP lookups under 25 ms p99.

05
STAGE 05 · SYSTEM DESIGN APPLICATIONSL7 · STAFF / PRINCIPAL

Where connection pool sizing and bulkheads protect backends.

Database connection governance is a universal bottleneck in microservice fleets:

  • Multi-Pod Kubernetes Deployments: Pair small per-pod HikariCP pools with PgBouncer/RDS Proxy transaction pooling.
  • OLTP vs. Reporting Separation: Always isolate ad-hoc search/export queries onto dedicated replicas and pools.
  • Circuit Breakers + Pool Timeouts: Trip Open when HikariPool wait times spike.
