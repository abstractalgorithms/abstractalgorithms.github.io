The idea to keep with you

When traffic spikes expose hidden coupling, keeping 100M 30-day jobs in RAM is prohibitively expensive; storing time-indexed keys in RocksDB shards while loading only the 15-minute lookahead horizon into a RAM Time Wheel gives O(1) firing at < 500 ms jitter.

TL;DR30-second simplified summary
▾

Each shard's Raft leader maintains a RAM Time Wheel fed by a 15-minute RocksDB lookahead scanner. Near-term writes dual-write to WAL and RAM.

Visibility leases and idempotency keys ensure at-least-once delivery, while failed jobs self-schedule with exponential backoff + jitter.

A Hierarchical Time Wheel stacks multiple circular ring buffers at increasing time granularities (ms -> s -> m -> h) to achieve O(1) timer insertion and cancellation.

STAGE 01 · STORAGE & RAM SIZINGfoundation

Separating 30-Day Disk Storage from 15-Minute RAM Wheels.

Why this matters: Size 100M pending 30-day jobs (100 GB disk) vs. a 15-minute RAM Time Wheel (1.4 GB) at 50K schedules/sec.

Assume 1 KB average job payload: 100M * 1 KB = 100 GB total pending state. At 50K schedules/sec, 15 minutes of near-term jobs is 50K * 900s = 45M lightweight (fire_at_ms, job_id) pointers (~1.4 GB across 16 shards = ~90 MB/shard).

Distant jobs stay compressed on SSD in RocksDB keyed by fire_at_bucket || job_id.

STAGE 01 · STORAGE & RAM SIZING

Separating 30-Day Disk Storage from 15-Minute RAM Wheels

1.Memory Math: 45M (fire_at_ms, job_id) pointers across 16 shards fit in ~1.4 GB RAM instead of 100 GB.

2.30-Day Horizon: Distant schedules never bloat RAM or garbage collectors.

3.p99 Firing Jitter: Combining O(1) Time Wheel buckets with non-empty DelayQueue wakeups holds jitter <500 ms.

Separating 100 GB of 30-day RocksDB state from a 1.4 GB 15-minute RAM Time Wheel sustains 50K schedules/sec.

02
STAGE 02 · SHARDING & LEADER ELECTIONfoundation

Partitioned RocksDB Shards with Raft Leader Leases.

Why this matters: Partition state into 32 Raft-replicated RocksDB shards where only the active shard leader runs the RAM wheel.

Partition into 32 virtual shards replicated across 3 nodes via Raft (or Kafka + standby). Only the active Raft leader for a shard runs the RAM Time Wheel and dispatches timers.

On leader failure, the newly elected leader scans [last_acked_cursor, now + 15m] from RocksDB into a fresh Time Wheel in < 2 seconds.

STAGE 02 · SHARDING & LEADER ELECTION

Partitioned RocksDB Shards with Raft Leader Leases

1.32 Virtual Shards: Sharding keeps per-node RAM wheels small and spreads write IOPS across the cluster.

2.Single-Leader Dispatch: Only the Raft leader fires timers for its shard, preventing duplicate dispatches.

3.Standby Replicas: Followers replicate the RocksDB WAL synchronously and rebuild the 15m RAM wheel on promotion.

32 Raft-replicated RocksDB shards ensure durable writes and clean single-leader Time Wheel ownership.

STAGE 03 · LOOKAHEAD ENGINEintermediate

Streaming Near-Term Buckets into the Hierarchical Time Wheel.

Why this matters: Stream near-term buckets into the Time Wheel using a 15-minute lookahead watermark.

Maintain a loaded_horizon_ms watermark. Every 5 seconds, a background loader scans RocksDB [loaded_horizon_ms, now + 15m] and inserts pointers into the Time Wheel. Jobs arriving with fire_at <= now + 15m insert into BOTH RocksDB and the RAM Time Wheel immediately.

class ShardScheduler:
    def __init__(self, wal_store, time_wheel, dispatch_queue) -> None:
        self.wal = wal_store
        self.wheel = time_wheel
        self.dispatch_queue = dispatch_queue
        self.horizon_ms = 0

def schedule(self, job_id: str, fire_at_ms: int, payload: bytes, now_ms: int) -> None:
        if not job_id:
            raise ValueError('job_id is required')
        self.wal.put_idempotent(fire_at_ms, job_id, payload)
        if fire_at_ms <= self.horizon_ms or fire_at_ms <= now_ms + 900_000:
            self.wheel.add_task(fire_at_ms, job_id)
STAGE 03 · LOOKAHEAD ENGINE

Streaming Near-Term Buckets into the Hierarchical Time Wheel

1.No Missed Short Jobs: New jobs due within 15 minutes write to RocksDB AND insert directly into the RAM Time Wheel.

2.Duplicate Guard: Track a per-job state (PENDING -> LOADED -> IN_FLIGHT) to avoid double-loading at boundary edges.

3.Bounded Scan IOPS: Keying RocksDB by (fire_at_ms, job_id) makes lookahead scans sequential O(1) disk reads.

A time-ordered RocksDB keyspace and 5-second lookahead scans stream near-term jobs into RAM with zero missed short timers.

STAGE 04 · IDEMPOTENT DISPATCHintermediate

Two-Phase ACK, Lease Timeouts & Idempotency Keys.

Why this matters: Guarantee at-least-once delivery with 30s visibility leases, two-phase ACKs, and downstream Idempotency-Keys.

When a bucket fires, move jobs to IN_FLIGHT with a 30-second visibility lease and push (job_id, attempt_no, idempotency_key) to the Worker Pool. Workers call the downstream webhook/payment API with Idempotency-Key.

If the worker ACKs, tombstone the job in RocksDB; if the 30s lease expires, reschedule with exponential backoff + jitter.

STAGE 04 · IDEMPOTENT DISPATCH

Two-Phase ACK, Lease Timeouts & Idempotency Keys

1.Visibility Lease: If a worker crashes mid-HTTP call, the 30s lease expires and another worker retries safely.

2.Two-Phase ACK: Never delete a timer when it fires from the wheel—only after the worker ACKs completion.

3.End-to-End Idempotency: Passing a deterministic Idempotency-Key prevents duplicate charges on rare lease expirations.

30s visibility leases, two-phase ACKs, and Idempotency-Key headers deliver reliable at-least-once job execution.

STAGE 05 · RETRIES & DLQadvanced

Self-Scheduling Exponential Backoff & Dead-Letter Queues.

Why this matters: Handle transient 5xx/429 failures via self-scheduling exponential backoff and dead-letter queues.

Think of Self-Scheduling Exponential Backoff & Dead-Letter Queues as the intuition layer for Design a Distributed Delay Queue & Retry Timer Service with Hierarchical Time Wheels: Handle transient 5xx/429 failures via self-scheduling exponential backoff and dead-letter queues.

When attempt k fails with a retryable 5xx/429, the worker re-enqueues job_id into the same Delay Queue with delay = min(max_delay, base * 2^k) + uniform_jitter. After max_retries (e.g., 8 attempts) or a permanent 4xx error, route the payload + failure trace to a Dead-Letter Queue (DLQ) for operator replay.

STAGE 05 · RETRIES & DLQ

Self-Scheduling Exponential Backoff & Dead-Letter Queues

1.Dogfooding the Wheel: Using the same Delay Queue for exponential retries requires zero extra retry infrastructure.

2.Full Jitter: Randomizing backoff prevents synchronized retry waves from hammering recovering downstream APIs.

3.DLQ Replay API: Expose a replay endpoint so operators can re-drive quarantined jobs after fixing bugs.

Self-scheduled exponential backoff with jitter and tenant DLQs handle transient and permanent failures cleanly.

Handling Midnight-UTC Cron Spikes & Hot Tenant Shards
