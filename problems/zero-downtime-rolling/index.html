The idea to keep with you

To keep the architecture evolvable without sacrificing reliability, coordinate Readiness probes, preStop sleep, and HTTP keep-alive Connection: close headers.

TL;DR30-second simplified summary
▾

Even when apps drain in-flight requests, long-lived HTTP keep-alive connections and async kube-proxy updates cause 502s. Flipping Readiness to 503, sleeping in preStop, and closing keep-alive sockets after active responses eliminates rollout errors.

Engineering Decision Framework
Problem
How to implement Eliminating 502 Races in Rolling Deploys without hitting unbounded tail latency, state divergence, or operational fragility.
Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
To keep the architecture evolvable without sacrificing reliability, coordinate Readiness probes, preStop sleep, and HTTP keep-alive Connection: close headers.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.
