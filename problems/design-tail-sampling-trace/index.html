The idea to keep with you

TL;DR30-second simplified summary
▾

At 1,000,000 spans/second (~1.5 GB/s raw OTLP, ~130 TB/day), storing every span in ClickHouse or Tempo is financially prohibitive, yet 1% head-based sampling blinds on-call engineers during Sev-1 incidents. Designing a 1M-Span/Sec Distributed Tail-Based Sampling Pipeline requires solving stateful trace assembly at scale without OOMKilling collectors or impacting live microservice latency.

The architecture splits ingestion into four stages: (1) non-blocking OTel SDKs in microservices propagating W3C traceparent; (2) stateless Tier 1 Edge Collectors that strip oversized payload attributes and route spans via consistent hashing on trace_id; (3) Tier 2 Stateful Tail-Sampling Collectors (16 pods x 4 GB RAM) holding a 30-second trace buffer with 80% memory_limiter breakers, evaluating 100% errors + p99 > 500 ms + VIP debug tenants + 1% healthy; and (4) a Kafka shock-absorber topic feeding batched 100K-row inserts into ClickHouse—cutting storage by 95% (~6.5 TB/day) while preserving 100% incident visibility.

Sizing 1M Spans/Sec, 45 GB Collector RAM & 95% Storage Savings.

Why this matters: Size raw OTLP ingress (1.5 GB/s), 30s Tier 2 RAM buffers (~45 GB), and post-sampling ClickHouse storage.

1,000,000 spans/sec at ~1.5 KB/span = 1.5 GB/s raw ingress (~130 TB/day un-sampled).

Buffering 30 seconds (decision_wait) requires 30M spans x 1.5 KB = ~45 GB active RAM across 16 Tier 2 pods (~4 GB RAM each with headroom).

STAGE 01 · CAPACITY & STORAGE MATH

Sizing 1M Spans/Sec, 45 GB Collector RAM & 95% Storage Savings

1.130 TB -> 6.5 TB/Day: Tail sampling cuts daily ClickHouse ingest by 20x without losing a single error trace.

2.RAM Sizing Formula: Collector RAM = (spans_per_sec * decision_wait_s * bytes_per_span) * 1.4 headroom.

3.Attribute Stripping: Dropping >2 KB stack/body tags at Tier 1 shrinks 45 GB buffer requirements further.

16 Tier 2 pods (4 GB each) buffer 30s of 1M spans/sec and reduce ClickHouse ingest from 130 TB/day to 6.5 TB/day.

STAGE 02 · TWO-TIER PIPELINEfoundation

Stateless Tier 1 Trace-ID Routers -> Stateful Tier 2 Collectors -> Kafka.

Why this matters: Separate stateless OTLP ingestion/routing from stateful 30s trace assembly and decoupled Kafka -> ClickHouse persistence.

Apps export OTLP/gRPC to local node DaemonSets or Tier 1 stateless collectors, never blocking request threads.

Tier 1 hashes trace_id onto a consistent-hash ring of Tier 2 pods. Tier 2 buffers spans, evaluates rules at t + 30s, and publishes kept traces to Kafka.

STAGE 02 · TWO-TIER PIPELINE

Stateless Tier 1 Trace-ID Routers -> Stateful Tier 2 Collectors -> Kafka

1.Zero App Coupling: Application pods never wait on Tier 2 or ClickHouse; full SDK buffers drop telemetry silently.

2.Tier 1 Stateless Scale: Autoscale Tier 1 freely on CPU without disrupting Tier 2 trace_id affinity.

3.Kafka Buffer: Absorbs ClickHouse maintenance or bursty incident error-storms cleanly.

Two-tier trace_id routing plus a Kafka buffer isolates app latency, preserves trace assembly, and protects ClickHouse.

STAGE 03 · SAFE IMPLEMENTATIONintermediate

Consistent-Hash Trace Router & Bounded Memory Tail Buffer.

Why this matters: Implement trace_id consistent-hash routing with unhealthy-node failover and bounded trace eviction.

Use Jump Consistent Hash or Ketama virtual nodes so adding/removing a Tier 2 collector remaps only 1/N traces.

import hashlib

class TraceRingRouter:
    def __init__(self, pods: list[str], vnodes: int = 100) -> None:
        if not pods:
            raise ValueError('At least one Tier 2 pod is required')
        self.ring: list[tuple[int, str]] = []
        for pod in pods:
            for v in range(vnodes):
                h = int(hashlib.md5(f'{pod}#{v}'.encode()).hexdigest()[:8], 16)
                self.ring.append((h, pod))
        self.ring.sort()

    def route_trace(self, trace_id: str) -> str:
        if not trace_id:
            raise ValueError('trace_id cannot be empty')
        h = int(hashlib.md5(trace_id.encode()).hexdigest()[:8], 16)
        for ring_h, pod in self.ring:
            if h <= ring_h:
                return pod
        return self.ring[0][1]
STAGE 03 · SAFE IMPLEMENTATION

Consistent-Hash Trace Router & Bounded Memory Tail Buffer

1.100 Virtual Nodes: 100 vnodes per collector pod keep span distribution within ~5% variance across the ring.

2.1/N Remapping: Scaling from 16 to 17 pods only splits ~6% of active 30s traces instead of 94% with modulo.

3.Health-Check Skip: If a Tier 2 pod fails readiness probes, walk clockwise to the next healthy pod.

100-vnode consistent hashing keeps trace routing O(log V) and limits ring churn during autoscaling.

STAGE 04 · SCALING & HOT-TRACE SHEDDINGintermediate

Handling 500K-Span Mega-Traces & Error-Storm Surges.

Why this matters: Prevent Sev-1 error storms (when kept rate jumps from 5% to 80%) from overwhelming Kafka and ClickHouse.

During a major database outage, error rate jumps from 1% to 80%—causing tail sampling to keep 16x more traces than normal!

Apply a per-policy rate-limiting sampler (rate_limiting / probabilistic cap inside and composite policy) so error traces are capped at e.g. 50,000 spans/sec per service during a Sev-1 storm—still plenty for debugging without melting ClickHouse.

STAGE 04 · SCALING & HOT-TRACE SHEDDING

Handling 500K-Span Mega-Traces & Error-Storm Surges

1.Error-Storm Trap: Keeping 100% of errors unconditionally during a 100%-error outage turns tail sampling into 100% ingestion.

2.Per-Service Error Cap: Capping kept errors per service ensures 1 failing service doesn't crowd out other services' traces.

3.Debuggable Volume: 50,000 error spans/sec still gives on-call engineers thousands of full examples per minute.

Per-service error rate caps and 10K-span trace limits keep ClickHouse queryable during Sev-1 error storms.

05
STAGE 05 · FAULT ISOLATIONadvanced

Memory_Limiter Circuit Breakers & Per-Tenant Span Quotas.

Why this matters: Enforce 80% memory_limiter breakers and Tier 1 tenant quotas so noisy services never OOM collectors.

A single buggy tenant logging 50 KB payloads in span attributes can exhaust Tier 2 RAM.

Configure Tier 1 transform processor to truncate attributes >1 KB and enforce per-tenant token buckets; configure Tier 2 memory_limiter at 80% hard limit.

STAGE 05 · FAULT ISOLATION

Memory_Limiter Circuit Breakers & Per-Tenant Span Quotas

1.Attribute Bloat Prevention: Truncating oversized SQL/JSON tags at Tier 1 keeps span size predictable (~1.5 KB).

2.Noisy-Neighbor Isolation: Per-service span quotas prevent one chatty microservice from starving the entire company's traces.

3.Graceful Refusal: Refusing spans at 80% RAM preserves already-buffered traces instead of losing all 30s on OOMKill.

Tier 1 attribute truncation, per-service quotas, and 80% memory_limiter breakers prevent collector OOMKills.

06
STAGE 06 · RECOVERY & DEPLOYSadvanced

Two-Phase Ring Draining & Kafka Replay Safety.

Why this matters: Drain Tier 2 collectors cleanly over decision_wait during upgrades and replay Kafka batches idempotently.

Rolling updates on Tier 2 must not drop 30 seconds of in-flight traces.

Remove the pod from Tier 1 service endpoints, sleep decision_wait + 5s (35s) in preStop while evaluating remaining traces, and flush to Kafka before SIGTERM.

STAGE 06 · RECOVERY & DEPLOYS

Two-Phase Ring Draining & Kafka Replay Safety

1.35s Graceful Drain: 5s buffer over 30s decision_wait guarantees the last buffered trace evaluates and exports.

2.Kafka Retention: 72-hour Kafka retention lets ClickHouse survive multi-hour schema migrations without losing traces.

3.Slow HPA Cooldown: Use 5-10 minute scale-in cooldowns on Tier 2 to avoid frequent hash-ring churn.

35s preStop ring draining and post-ACK Kafka offset commits guarantee zero trace loss during deploys.

60s synthetic error canaries, rootless-trace alerts, and kept-ratio FinOps caps keep the pipeline reliable.
