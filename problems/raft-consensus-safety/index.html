The idea to keep with you

When a single bottleneck threatens end-to-end availability, never commit log entries from a previous term by counting replicas alone (Figure 8 bug); a leader may only advance `commitIndex` for entries from its `currentTerm`, and linearizable reads require `ReadIndex` quorum heartbeats.

Split-Brain Prevention
Split-brain happens when a network partition splits a distributed cluster into isolated groups that both elect a primary and accept conflicting writes; preventing it requires strict majority quorums (N/2 + 1), time-bounded leases, and monotonic fencing tokens.

The Raft Figure 8 anomaly and stale reads from deposed leaders.

Why this matters: The problem: see what breaks without The Raft Figure 8 anomaly and stale reads from deposed leaders under real concurrency or scale.

Two classic bugs break Raft State Machine Safety:

  1. Figure 8 Overwrite: Leader S1 (term 2) replicates entry k to {S1, S2, S3} and crashes before committing it. S5 (which missed k, but had a higher-term entry from term 3) wins election in term 4 and overwrites k—even if k was already on a majority!
  2. Deposed Leader Stale Reads: A partitioned old leader continues serving its local in-memory state machine before its heartbeat timeout fires.
Raft Figure 8 & ReadIndex

Why Previous-Term Entries Cannot Commit by Replica Count Alone

A leader may only advance commitIndex for entries from its currentTerm; older-term entries commit only indirectly.

Naive Solution
Use a single unpartitioned path with unbounded retries and synchronous global coordination.
Why It Fails
Global coordination serializes throughput under load, while unbounded retries amplify transient stalls into full outages.
Alternatives
Compare synchronous strict-consistency paths against bounded asynchronous replication, local caching, or partitioned ownership.
Engineering Decision
When a single bottleneck threatens end-to-end availability, never commit log entries from a previous term by counting replicas alone (Figure 8 bug); a leader may only advance commitIndex for entries from its currentTerm, and linearizable reads require ReadIndex quorum heartbeats.
Trade-Offs
Gains predictable p99 latency and fault isolation in exchange for explicit bounding, observability, and slightly higher implementation complexity.

Skipping fsync() on currentTerm, votedFor, and log[] before replying to RequestVote or AppendEntries allows a rebooted node to vote twice in the same term (two leaders in one term). Likewise, serving GET directly from the leader's in-memory map without a quorum check returns stale data during asymmetric partitions.

  • Majority quorum prevents split-brain
  • Fencing tokens reject zombies

04
STAGE 04 · ENTERPRISE SOLUTIONL6 · SCALE & TRADE-OFFS

Enterprise solution: Five Raft Invariants, currentTerm Commit, and ReadIndex.

Why this matters: Operational control: configure bounds, failure handling, and safe production defaults.

Production Raft engines (etcd/tikv) enforce strict safety with bounded 150–300 ms randomized election timeouts and 50 ms heartbeats:

  • Log Up-to-Date Election Restriction: RequestVote rejects any candidate whose (lastLogTerm, lastLogIndex) is behind the voter's.
  • currentTerm Commit Rule: Leader advances commitIndex only when log[N].term == currentTerm and a majority (3/5) has persisted it (fsync 2–5 ms batched).
  • Linearizable ReadIndex (Zero Disk Writes): Leader records readIndex = commitIndex, sends a heartbeat round to confirm quorum, waits for lastApplied >= readIndex, and serves the read locally.
Raft Node Roles

Follower, Candidate, and Leader Safety Transitions

Randomized 150-300ms election timeouts avoid split votes while ReadIndex guarantees linearizable reads without disk writes.

STAGE 05 · SYSTEM DESIGN APPLICATIONSL7 · STAFF / PRINCIPAL

Where Raft consensus powers critical infrastructure.

Why this matters: Scope boundary: identify where this pattern fits best and where simpler or async alternatives win.

Raft is the standard consensus protocol for strongly consistent control planes and sharded storage:

  • Kubernetes etcd & Consul: Stores cluster state, service discovery, and leader leases.
  • Distributed Locks & Fencing Tokens: Generates monotonically increasing term + index fencing tokens.
  • TiKV / CockroachDB (Multi-Raft): Runs thousands of independent Raft groups per node for horizontal scale.
