Deep Dive: Stage 2 — Clustering Algorithms: K-Means, DBSCAN, and Hierarchical

  • K-Means alternates two steps until convergence: 1.
  • Assign each point to the nearest centroid.
  • Recompute each centroid as the mean of its assigned points.
  • K-Means minimizes: $$\min {{c k}} \sum {i=1}^{n} \ x i - c {k(i)} \ ^2$$ It is fast but assumes roughly spherical clusters and requires choosing $k$ in advance.

Deep Dive: Stage 3 — Deep Dive: How K-Means Converges Step by Step

  • K-Means alternates two steps until assignments stop changing.
  • In the assignment step , each point is assigned to the nearest centroid by Euclidean distance.
  • In the update step , each centroid moves to the mean of its assigned points.
  • Total inertia (within-cluster sum of squares) can only decrease or stay flat each iteration — it never increases — guaranteeing convergence, though not necessarily to the global optimum.

Deep Dive: Stage 4 — Dimensionality Reduction: PCA, t-SNE, and Autoencoders

  • PCA (Principal Component Analysis): - Linear technique — finds the directions of maximum variance in the data.
    • Great baseline for tabular numeric data.
    • "Variance captured by first 2 components = 87%" is easy to communicate.
  • t-SNE and UMAP: - Non-linear embeddings that preserve neighborhood relationships.

Deep Dive: Stage 5 — Real-World Applications: Unsupervised Learning in Production

  • Customer segmentation: cluster purchase behavior to identify high-value, at-risk, and new users.
  • Feed cluster labels into targeted marketing campaigns.
  • Anomaly detection: DBSCAN marks sparse points as noise — those are your anomalies in log data or sensor streams.
  • Search and recommendation: PCA or autoencoders compress item feature vectors into dense embeddings.
